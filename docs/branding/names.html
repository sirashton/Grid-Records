Name ideas

Twenty names in six territories, each shown as a lockup with the chosen mark (3f-a Teal) and a one-line tagline, and in a browser tab with its favicon. One sentence each on why it works and where it is weak, including whether it travels to grids where the last fossil fuel is coal rather than gas. The Long Run stays at the top as the current favourite; the top three are at the end.

Duration and endurance

How long the grid can keep going without fossil fuel. The current favourite lives here.

The Long Run
How long grids run without fossil fuels, and the records along the way.
The Long Run
New tab
+
Current favourite

Says both the thing being measured (how long a run lasts) and the direction of travel (the long run of the transition), and it travels to every country. Weakness: a common phrase, shared with podcasts and finance columns, so it needs the tagline and the mark to be unmistakably about the grid.

Longest Spell
The longest spells without coal and gas, grid by grid.
Longest Spell
New tab
+

Concrete and exactly what the records page shows, with the site's own unit in the name. Weakness: it points at the past record rather than the one happening now, and "spell" reads a little British.

Unbroken
Unbroken runs without fossil fuel, and how long they last.
Unbroken
New tab
+

One confident word for a streak that has not been interrupted, and it hints at a grid that holds. Weakness: better known as a film and a book title, and on its own it does not say "electricity".

Reliability and firmness

A grid you can count on without burning anything. Alex asked for something about being more reliable.

Firm Ground
A grid that stands on wind, water, sun and storage.
Firm Ground
New tab
+

"Firm" is the industry's word for power you can rely on, so this claims reliability for the low-carbon mix without saying "renewable". Weakness: sounds a touch like a consultancy or an insurer.

Steady State
How steadily grids now run without coal and gas.
Steady State
New tab
+

An engineering term that means exactly the right thing: a system holding its balance without intervention. Weakness: generic enough to be used by dozens of products, and hard to own in search.

The Hold
How long the grid holds without gas.
The Hold
New tab
+

Short, calm, and the metaphor is right: the clean mix holding the load, hour after hour. Weakness: vague out of context, and "hold" has wrestling and telephone meanings before it has grid ones.

Baseload
Wind, sun, water and batteries are the new baseload.
Baseload
New tab
+

Deliberately provocative: it takes the word gas and coal plants have always hidden behind and hands it to the clean grid. Weakness: contested jargon, and the name is already used by energy companies and newsletters.

The endgame for coal and gas

The other thing the site is really about: the last years of the fossil fleet.

Last Burn
Counting down the last coal and gas on the grid.
Last Burn
New tab
+

Editorial and a little dramatic, and it works wherever the fuel differs: the last coal in Poland and Australia, the last gas in Britain. Weakness: the drama tips toward morbid, and it says nothing about records or durations.

Closing Time
The last years of coal and gas, grid by grid.
Closing Time
New tab
+

Everyone knows what closing time means and it fits a site that watches plants retire. Weakness: a famous song title and a pub phrase first, and it frames the site as an obituary rather than a scoreboard.

Fade Out
Coal and gas fading from the grid, measured every half hour.
Fade Out
New tab
+

Describes the mark itself: the orange thinning row by row. Short and travels anywhere. Weakness: "fade" is a soft word for a hard transition, and it could be read as the site itself fading.

After Gas
What the British grid does when the gas plants are off.
After Gas
New tab
+

Blunt and right for the UK story, where gas is the last fossil fuel standing. Weakness: does not travel to Germany, Poland or Australia, where coal is the one on the way out.

The records grids keep setting on the way off fossil fuels.
Grid Records

+

The incumbent: plain, descriptive, searchable, and still accurate across every country. Weakness: it is a description more than a name, and it will never be the thing people quote.

New Record

New Record

Plain and descriptive

Names that need no explaining, at the cost of character.

Hours the grid ran without coal or gas, counted.
Fossil-Free Hours
New tab
+

Exactly the unit of the site, and it works in every country. Weakness: long, hyphenated and dry, and "fossil-free" is already a campaign phrase.

Clean Hours
Hours of clean power, and the records they set.
Clean Hours
New tab
+

Shorter than the above and still says what is counted. Weakness: "clean" invites arguments about biomass and nuclear, and it is a common product name.

Wildcards

Further out, for range.

Zero Hour
The hours with zero coal and gas, and how many in a row.
Zero Hour
New tab
+

The hours with zero fossil output, and a countdown feel for the fleet. Weakness: the military sense arrives first for some readers, and it is a well-used title.

Quiet Grid
A grid where the big fossil plants have gone quiet.
Quiet Grid
New tab
+

Captures the mood of a gas-free night: the big plants silent, nothing burning. Weakness: quiet is a strange promise for a live data site, and it says nothing about records.

Tailwind
The transition is picking up: records, durations, the end of coal and gas.
Tailwind
New tab
+

Optimistic, one word, and the wind pun is quiet enough to be allowed. Weakness: Tailwind is a very well-known web framework, so search and social handles are gone.

Threshold
Records for how long grids stay below any level of coal and gas.
Threshold
New tab
+

The records page is literally about thresholds (how long the grid stays under a level), so it is precise. Weakness: abstract, and it would need the tagline every time.

Verdict

Top three

  1. The Long Run. Still the best fit. It names the measurement and the destination in three ordinary words, it is confident rather than clever, and it works unchanged in Britain, New Zealand, Germany, Poland or Australia. The lockup is the strongest on this page.
  2. Last Burn. If the site is to be about the endgame as much as the records, this is the sharpest way to say it, and it travels: last coal there, last gas here. Pair it with a records-flavoured tagline so it does not read as an obituary.
  3. Firm Ground. The best of the reliability territory: it borrows the industry's own word for dependable power and quietly claims it for the clean mix. Softer than the other two, but it answers the "more reliable" brief directly.

Grid Records remains the safe fallback: nobody will misunderstand it, and nobody will remember it either.
