Name ideas, round two

The new test is findability: could someone Google the name one day and land here? So this round favours coinages, compounds and energy terms used freshly over everyday phrases. Each name has a lockup with the chosen mark, a browser-tab mock, a sentence on fit, then a search check (what a web search for the exact phrase finds today) and a rating.

Shortlist so far

The two names Alex has in hand, with honest findability notes.

The Long Run
How long grids run without fossil fuels, and the records along the way.
The Long Run
New tab
+

Still the best-fitting name on the page: the measurement and the direction of travel in three plain words, and it works everywhere. Weakness: it is an everyday phrase, so the site would be fighting for the search result for years.

Findability: Low

Search check. An Eagles album, a Zwift training article, several books and podcasts, and finance columns. The exact phrase is owned by everyone and no one; thelongrun.com will be long gone.

Last Burn
Counting down the last coal and gas on the grid.
Last Burn
New tab
+

Sharp, editorial, and it travels: the last coal in Poland or Australia, the last gas in Britain. Weakness: a little grim, and it says nothing about records or durations without the tagline.

Findability: Medium

Search check. No owner. Results are scattered: "last burnt" fire-history datasets, last.fm band pages, a novel called "What We'll Burn Last". Nothing big to displace.

Duration and endurance

Longspell

Longspell
New tab
+

One invented word that says exactly what the records are, and it looks like a name rather than a phrase. Weakness: "spell" reads as weather or magic before it reads as electricity.

Gridspell
How long each grid can run without fossil fuel.
Gridspell
New tab
+

Grid plus the unit: the most descriptive of the coinages, and unmistakably about electricity. Weakness: slightly clunky to say, and the two words together could suggest a word game.

Findability: High

Search check. No owner. Results are spellcheckers inside grid controls (Telerik, Syncfusion) and a word-jumble app called Spell Grid. Nothing to compete with.

Gasless
Gasless hours on the British grid, and the records they set.
Gasless
New tab
+

Blunt, one word, and immediately understood in Britain. Weakness: UK-centric (coal is the fuel going in Poland and Australia) and a plain adjective, so Google will keep treating it as a word.

Findability: Medium

Search check. No owner, but generic: results are gas-provider apps and "gas-free" articles. Ownable only with the grid context attached.

The end of coal and gas

Names for the fleet retiring, built to be distinctive.

Unfired
The hours when nothing on the grid is burning.
Unfired
New tab
+

A real word used freshly: an unfired grid is one where no boiler is lit. Quiet, confident, travels to any fuel. Weakness: also means pottery before the kiln, and "unfired" has the ghost of being sacked.

Findability: Medium

Search check. No owner. Results are fire-damaged music studios and a company called Unifire. The word is free.

Gasfall
Gas falling off the grid, measured every half hour.
Gasfall
New tab
+

Built like nightfall: a coinage for the slow fall of gas generation, and it looks like a place name. Weakness: UK-centric, and Google currently reads it as "gas prices fall".

Findability: High

Search check. No owner. Results are gas-price news and Vattenfall's gas tariffs. Ownable with use.

Coalfall
The fall of coal, and what runs the grid after it.
Coalfall
New tab
+

The same coinage for the countries where coal is the last fossil fuel: Germany, Poland, Australia. Weakness: wrong word for Britain, where coal has already gone, so it would need to be the sibling of Gasfall rather than the name.

Findability: Medium

Search check. Nearly free. Coalfalls is a small suburb of Ipswich, Queensland; otherwise mining-history archives. Nothing big.

Last Gas

Records and milestones

The scoreboard, as a name you could own.

Grid Records
The records grids keep setting on the way off fossil fuels.
Grid Records
New tab
+

The incumbent: plain, accurate in every country, and it already says "records". Weakness: a description more than a name, and a record label uses it.

Findability: Medium

Search check. A small Bandcamp label called Grid Records, a "Modular Grid Records" on Resident Advisor, and DataFlex documentation. Not dominated, but not empty.

Greenspell

Greenspell
New tab
+

The low-carbon spell in one word; friendly and obviously about clean energy. Weakness: "green" is the most overused word in the sector, and the spelling-program noise in search will persist.

Search check. No owner. Results are a "Green Spelling Program", the gspell software package and a Wiccan spell book. Free but noisy.

Ember Count
New tab

Spindown

Spindown
New tab
+

The sound of a thermal plant going off: a spindown. Fresh in the energy sense and it travels. Weakness: a disk-drive and video-game term first, and it describes decline more than records.

Findability: Medium

Verdict

Top three for findability and fit

  1. Longspell. The best balance on the page: a one-word coinage with nothing in the way of it in search, built on the site's own unit, and it reads as a name in the tab and the lockup. It works for every country because a spell is a spell whatever the fuel.
  2. Last Burn. The strongest story name survives the search check well enough: no owner, just noise. It says the endgame better than anything else here and travels to coal and gas alike. Pair it with a records-flavoured tagline.
  3. Unfired. The sleeper. A real word nobody has used for this, free in search, quiet and confident, and it covers coal, gas and anything else that burns. Needs the tagline on first meeting, then it sticks.

Gridspell is the safer cousin of Longspell if "spell" on its own feels too far from electricity; Gasfall and Fossilfade are the most unique strings but the weakest words.
