      About the data

      Generation comes from the open data of ONS, the operator of Brazil's National Interconnected System (SIN): hourly average output for every plant it dispatches or monitors since 2000 ("Geração por Usina em Base Horária"). Since 2023 ONS also estimates the output of small distributed generation (MMGD), almost all of it rooftop and small ground-mounted solar; before then that solar is missing, not zero. Isolated systems off the national grid, mostly in the Amazon, are not included. Itaipu counts in full, including the half that belongs to Paraguay, most of which Brazil buys.

      Renewables here are hydro, wind, solar and biomass (mostly sugarcane bagasse). Nuclear is the two Angra reactors. Fossil fuels are gas, coal, oil and diesel, and "other thermal": plants ONS lists as multi-fuel or burning industrial waste gases.

      "Held back" is ONS's own estimate of the wind and solar output not generated because it limited the plants (generation not realised, GNRa, in its constrained-off data), from October 2021 for wind and April 2024 for solar. It counts output lost to grid limits, reliability limits and, since 2023 mostly, to there being more power than the system could use. It is not generation, so it is not counted in totals.

      A spell is a run of consecutive hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. ONS keys its data to Brasília time, which kept daylight saving until 2019, so one hour each February before then is missing. Years run in Brasília time.
