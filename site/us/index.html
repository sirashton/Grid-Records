      About the data

      Generation comes from the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Hourly Electric Grid Monitor (form EIA-930), through its six-month bulk files. Every balancing authority in the lower 48 reports its net generation by fuel each hour; this page adds them up, as EIA's own US48 totals do. Where EIA publishes an adjusted value (gaps and outliers cleaned), that is used, else the reported one. Alaska and Hawaii are not in EIA-930, and neither is rooftop solar.

      Each balancing authority is cleaned before the sum: a gap of up to six hours is filled in a straight line, a single hour that jumps far from both neighbours takes their mean, and a repeated row replaces the first. An hour is left out when balancing authorities making less than 97% of the usual output reported it, so one late report doesn't read as a 30 GW drop. History starts in October 2018, as PJM's fuel mix starts on 19 September 2018.

      Until June 2024 EIA-930 counted pumped storage in hydro, and batteries and geothermal in other. From July 2024 they are separate, with charging split out where a balancing authority reports it as negative output. Renewables here are wind, solar, hydro and geothermal; low carbon adds nuclear. Fossil fuels are gas, coal and oil. Net imports and exports are the flows with Canada and Mexico, what is left of interchange once flows between balancing authorities cancel.

      A spell is a run of consecutive hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. Years run in US Eastern time.
