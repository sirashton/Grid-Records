New Zealand already makes most of its electricity from water, steam and wind. This page follows the last part of the story: how long the grid can now run with almost no fossil fuel, and what it would take to go all the way.

    4Batteries are arrivingNew Zealand's first grid batteries connected from 2023.

    Battery output over time

    Daily mean battery discharge into the grid, in MW. The Electricity Authority's data shows what batteries send to the grid, not what they take when charging.

    The biggest is still to come. Genesis is building a battery at Huntly Power Station: 100 MW and 200 MWh due in 2026, and another 100 MW and 200 MWh around 2029 (Genesis to the NZX, ESS News). It plugs into the coal station's existing grid connection. At full size it covers about an eighth of the fossil capacity the grid leans on at its evening peaks, so the cost model in chapter 5 counts it. It does nothing for a dry year: 400 MWh lasts two hours, and a dry year is short of water for months.

    5When does 100% get cheaper?The cost of covering the driest year on record with renewables, against keeping coal and gas on standby.

    Getting rid of the last fossil fuel means covering the worst dry year without it. That takes enough extra renewable generation to replace the water the lakes don't get, and enough fast capacity to replace the gas and coal plants at the evening peaks. In wetter years, much of that extra energy isn't needed.

        About the data

        Generation comes from the Electricity Authority's generation output by plant: half-hourly metered output of every grid-connected plant since August 1997, published monthly. It leaves out rooftop solar and other small generation inside local networks, so solar and the total read lower than MBIE's national figures. Huntly's Rankine units can burn coal or gas, and the data counts them as coal.

        Renewables here are hydro, geothermal, wind, solar, wood and biogas. Fossil fuels are gas, coal and diesel. Battery discharge is shown on its own and is not counted in total generation.

        A spell is a run of consecutive half hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the half hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. Years run in New Zealand time.
