    About the data

    Generation comes from Fraunhofer ISE's Energy-Charts, through its open API: public net electricity generation in Germany every 15 minutes since 2015, from the grid operators' and the EEX transparency data. Each half hour here is the mean of its two quarter hours. "Public" means what is fed into the grid: it leaves out power that industry makes and uses on site, and the part of rooftop solar that homes use themselves, so solar and the total read lower than the national energy balance.

    Renewables here are solar, onshore and offshore wind, hydro and biomass (with about 20 MW of geothermal). Fossil fuels are lignite, hard coal, gas, and oil with gas from coke ovens and blast furnaces. Pumped storage counts as generation when it runs, and its pumping as a separate measure. Net imports are not counted in generation.

    A spell is a run of consecutive half hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the half hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. Years run in German time.
