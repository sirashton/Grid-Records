Grid Records

Australia's east coast grid still runs mostly on coal, but rooftop solar, wind and a fast-growing fleet of batteries are pushing it out. This page tracks how long the grid can now go with less and less coal and gas, and what keeps the rest running.

    The story

    About the data

    Generation comes from AEMO's MMS Data Model archive: the five-minute output of every scheduled and semi-scheduled unit in the National Electricity Market (Queensland, New South Wales and the ACT, Victoria, South Australia and Tasmania), averaged here to half hours. The weeks since AEMO's latest monthly archive come from its rolling NEMWEB reports (unit output, dispatch prices and demand, and rooftop solar), and are replaced by the archive once it is published. Each unit's fuel comes from AEMO's registration tables. Small non-scheduled plant, such as some older wind farms and landfill gas engines, is not in the unit data, so totals read a few percent lower than AEMO's own.

    Rooftop solar is AEMO's half-hourly estimate of household and business panels (ROOFTOP_PV_ACTUAL), counted as generation. Prices are each region's half-hourly spot price, and the whole-NEM price is weighted by each region's demand. Western Australia and the Northern Territory are separate grids; Western Australia has its own page.

    Renewables here are wind, solar farms, rooftop solar, hydro and bioenergy. Fossil fuels are black coal, brown coal, gas, and diesel and oil. Grid batteries are shown on their own, charging and discharging, and are not counted in total generation; pumped hydro's generation is counted as hydro. Home batteries are not in AEMO's data.

    A spell is a run of consecutive half hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the half hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. Times and years are in NEM time (AEST, UTC+10, no daylight saving).
