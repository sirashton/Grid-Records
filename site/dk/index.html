        About the data

        Generation, trade and consumption come from Energinet, the Danish grid operator, through its Energi Data Service (the GenerationProdTypeExchange dataset). It is hourly from 2015, for the two Danish bidding zones, DK1 (Jutland and Funen) and DK2 (Zealand, Lolland-Falster and Bornholm); this page adds the two together. Each hour carries the best figures Energinet has: first an "Initial" estimate scaled up from the larger plants' live readings, then settlement data after at least eight days, and a final version with that year's plant fuel counts by 1 June the next year.

        Thermal plants are counted by their main fuel, so a plant that burns mostly wood pellets but some coal counts as biomass. Waste incineration is shown on its own: part of what it burns is plastic, so it is neither fossil nor renewable here. Fossil fuels are coal, gas and oil. Renewables are onshore and offshore wind, solar, biomass and biogas, and hydro. Solar is what is fed into the grid; Energinet's estimate of rooftop solar used on site is not counted.

        Imports and exports are the net flow on the links to Norway, Sweden, Germany, the Netherlands and Great Britain, positive into Denmark, hour by hour; the Great Belt link between DK1 and DK2 is inside Denmark and left out. Prices are the day-ahead prices for DK1 and DK2, averaged, in euros per MWh: Elspotprices to September 2025, then DayAheadPrices.

        A spell is a run of consecutive hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. Years run in Danish time.
