      About the data

      Generation comes from Fraunhofer ISE's Energy-Charts, through its open API, which republishes the ENTSO-E transparency data that ESO, the Bulgarian grid operator, reports. Readings are hourly from 2015; each counts for its four quarter hours. "Public" generation is what is fed into the grid, so power that industry makes and uses on site, and the solar homes use themselves, is not counted.

      Batteries are in the data as "Battery" (discharge) and "Battery Consumption" (charging) only from 10 September 2026, although several gigawatts were running before that; they count as zero before. Until then, batteries at solar parks show up as "Solar" after dark (they discharge through the park's meter), and standalone ones only as a gap between load and everything listed, which is how Ember estimated them. Batteries and charging are shown on their own and are not counted in generation. Pumping at Chaira and the other pumped hydro plants is not reported, so pumped storage appears only when it generates.

      Renewables here are solar, onshore wind, hydro (run-of-river and reservoirs) and biomass. Low carbon adds nuclear. Fossil fuels are lignite, hard coal and gas. Net trade is the sum of the cross-border flows.

      Prices are the day-ahead prices for the Bulgarian bidding zone, from the same source, in euros per MWh: hourly, and every 15 minutes from October 2025. Installed capacity is Energy-Charts' yearly series.

      A spell is a run of consecutive quarter hours that meet the threshold. Gaps in the data of up to an hour are bridged when the quarter hours either side qualify; longer gaps end the spell. Years run in Bulgarian time.
